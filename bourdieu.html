05 · BRIDGING MACRO & MICRO

Pierre Bourdieu

Contemporary sociologists are concerned with how to bridge macro and micro:

  • How do structures influence the individual in daily lives
  • How do everyday interactions influence social structures

How social inequalities are reproduced through education, culture, and everyday practices.

Interested in class and power, but wanted to build a complex framework: inequality is not only about money, but also education, your taste, culture, everyday practices and habits.

Key concepts:

Habitus: how does society get inside us

  • How are our thoughts, instincts, tastes and behaviour shaped by society even without us noticing
  • Our social background shapes the way that we think, feel and live
  • We develop certain tastes, instincts, etc. through our upbringing and experiences
  • This “Habitus” becomes so natural to us, we don’t even have to think about it
Example: experiences of university students will vary greatly due to different ways of upbringing
A student whose parents went to university has a different experience than a first-generation university student.

Bourdieu uses this concept to bridge macro and micro sociology. He argues that habitus connects social structures to everyday experiences: social structures like institutions or family shape our habitus, and our habitus changes our way of acting and thinking, then through everyday behaviour we change the structures.

Example: social class and education
  • Social structures influence students’ experiences
  • Students grow up in different backgrounds and have different expectations of academics and pressure
  • Coming from a high socioeconomic background means you will have a different experience than someone from a lower social class
  • You will form habitus differently based on your social class background - someone from wealthier backgrounds might see university as a given while someone from working-class backgrounds might not, affecting how comfortable you feel

2 possibilities now:

  • You can reproduce the social structure -> adhere to expectations (parents went, I go too)
  • You can change the social structures -> first-generation students challenge it and change it
SOCIETY SHAPES US BUT WE ALSO SHAPE SOCIETY

Social field:

  • Rather than lumping all of society as a unit of analysis, it is made up of different social fields
  • Like a social arena that has its own rules and hierarchies, power, institutions, and values with people competing for resources and recognition

Examples: Education is its own social field | Sports is its own social field | Housing is its own social field

People compete within this field for resources, not necessarily in all of society. Performance matters in sports, not really in housing. This allows for meso analysis: macro is whole society and micro is people -> now we can see it in a broad sense and still specific.

4 Different types of capital (click to view):

People possess different types of capital that give different advantages in society (are generally interconvertable).

Economic Capital

How much money and material wealth you have (immediately convertible into money and institutionalized in property rights).

Cultural Capital: Things you have and know

  • Embodied: qualities of your mind and body (skills, looks, accent, postures, tastes, ...)
  • Objectified: material objects (vintage clothes, art, books - just not money)
  • Institutionalized: qualifications, credentials, social measures of capital (law degree, …)

Example: house -> economic capital transformed into cultural capital.

Social Capital: The people you know

  • Social networks and group memberships
  • Volume of Social Capital: size of network one can mobilize & capital possessed by each one
  • Requires unceasing, long-term effort of sociability (investing time, gifting presents)

Symbolic Capital (separate from the other three)

  • Recognition and legitimacy of other capital
  • Without symbolic capital, other capital would be insignificant
  • Example: you study in another country, get a degree, come to a Western country and it is not recognized -> the cultural capital loses its value without symbolic capital

Marx vs. Weber vs. Bourdieu

Marx: inequality works only on a money or monetary level.
Two classes placed on different levels (dominant vs. subordinate class).
Weber: there is stratification in the 3 Ps (Paper, Prestige, Power).
Bourdieu: without the meaning and recognition that we ascribe, these different forms of stratification would be irrelevant.
