03 · SYMBOLIC INTERACTIONISM

Symbolic Interactionism

Herbert Blumer:

  • Coined the term
  • Wrote a book about it
  • Rests on 3 premises:
(1) Human beings act toward things on the basis of the meanings they ascribe to those things
Simply put: Our behaviour towards social phenomena depends on how we define them
(2) Meanings that we ascribe to said phenomena come from social interaction
(3) Meanings are handled and modified through an interpretative process used by the person in dealing with things they encounter, therefore meanings can change (i.e. people must interpret meanings)

Critiques:

From Macro:

  • Too much of a narrow focus on meaning and interpretation
  • It doesn’t look at social structure and culture
  • Easy words: if you just look at interactions you miss things like policy or regime types, medical systems, …

These critiques between them reflect a prominent debate in sociology:

Referred to as the STRUCTURE AND AGENCY DEBATE

Are our behaviors governed by structure or rather agency?

Social structure: the underlying framework of society which is made up of predictable relationships.
The structures are outside of people’s control and influence them.
Agency: the freedom of individuals to choose and to act.
Example: sex work

Structure side: sex work is inherently oppressive because it is dictated by structures that oppress the women working in it (capitalist world for example). They are dominated by oppressive structures that dictate their life -> capitalist system that dictates their livelihood.

Agency side (symbolic interactionist perspective): women have the will to choose and exercise the work.

Symbolic Interactionist Perspectives:

  • In general the perspective dictates that we can learn about the social world through social relationships
  • It generalizes about everyday forms of social interactions to understand society as a whole
  • Suggests that our identity or sense of self is shaped by social interaction
  • We come to know ourselves by living with others

Labeling Theory:

  • Argues that a social condition or a group is viewed as problematic because it is labeled as such
Example: Is sexuality sinful?
Labeling theorists: only because it is labeled sinful. To change the meaning of something we have to change the label of it. It is not sinful, rather we make it sinful by saying it is sinful. To make it not sinful just say it is not.
Example: killing during war
The killing from soldiers in war is okay because we deem it okay, but other murder is not because we label it so.

Stigma (like a stereotype): a label used to devalue members of certain social groups

  • A brand or mark that is put on someone that hurts their social position
  • Example: if you label someone a criminal they carry that label everywhere with them in society
  • Criminologist sociologists: we should not label people criminals because it is not positive
  • Developed within symbolic interactionism by Erving Goffman
