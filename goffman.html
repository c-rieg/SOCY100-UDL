04 · DRAMATURGICAL APPROACH

Erving Goffman

  • Wrote The Presentation of Self in Everyday Life
  • The most cited work in contemporary sociology
  • The dramaturgical approach
  • You can look at people’s lives as if they were actors in a movie
  • Everybody has different masks and puts them on for different people and groups
  • There is no single true self, rather a type and masks that are put on

Main arguments in The Presentation of Self in Everyday Life:

Social interaction is characterized by individuals who perform on a front stage.

Front stage: how people see us and how we act in public.
Back stage: how we are when no one is around us.

When we engage with others we engage in IMPRESSION MANAGEMENT (on the front stage):

  • We alter behavior to promote an idealized version of ourselves
  • Uphold a public image and don’t let others see behind the stage

Humans interact according to social scripts or codes of behavior (impression management is based on social scripts):

  • We obey these rules and uphold them
  • We have them internalized and act according to them

Because of social scripts, people engage in face work: they wear masks to maintain image and avoid embarrassment.
